## 7. Custody Findings

The custody attack distinguishes:

```text
ContentIdentity:
  a claim or witness that identifies content, such as a digest.

ContentAvailability:
  the content bytes or representation can be accessed.

Recontactability:
  the content/evidence can be accessed again later under a stable reference.

Custody:
  governed preservation of recontactable evidence plus lineage.

CustodyAuthority:
  authority or standing to preserve, rely on, or promote that custody.
```

Minimum continuity requires:

```text
RecontactableEvidenceAvailability
+ identity binding
+ lineage sufficient for the bounded equivalence test
```

It does not require a specific storage technology. It does not require
`file_exists`. It does not require custody authority inside the continuity
theorem; custody authority belongs to governed participation or evidence
admission.

Hostile case:

```text
digest survives
original bytes disappear
derived summary survives
later contradiction appears
```

Remaining possible claims:



```text

```

Comparison:

```text

```

Permitted claims:

```text

  O1 occurred.
  O2 occurred.
  O1 conflicted with O2.
  W later favored Y.
  current standing may be Y without falsifying historical conflict.

B:
  current representation says Y.
  historical claim that O1 asserted X is lost.
  conflict audit is impossible.

C:
  current standing Y is visible.
  causal and epistemic history are unreconstructable.
```

Pressure:

```text

RECONSTRUCTION != RETROACTIVE_COHERENCE
```

Contradiction preservation is part of accountable causal continuity when
contradictory observations entered the trajectory. It may also be described as
an epistemic-history facet, but separating it out must not allow contradiction
erasure.
